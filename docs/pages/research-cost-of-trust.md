# The rising cost of trust in the age of <em>AI</em>

Why insurance must redesign trust itself.

## Summary

AI makes information cheap and verification expensive.

Self-Sovereign Identity (SSI) and KERI-based mechanisms allow insurers to shift from human-mediated trust to cryptographic proof — turning trust into reusable infrastructure.

## The hidden cost

For decades, insurance has quietly absorbed the **cost of establishing trust**.

It appears everywhere:

- Identity verification
- Claims assessment
- Fraud detection
- Compliance reporting
- Manual reviews
- Third-party checks
- Expert opinions

AI now threatens to multiply these costs by making synthetic evidence trivial to generate.

## The AI paradox

> The cheaper it becomes to generate information, the more expensive it becomes to verify it.

Fraud scales. Synthetic identities multiply. Operational friction rises.

Without structural change, insurers face:

- Slower claims handling
- Ballooning operating costs
- Rising customer friction
- Eroding margins

AI doesn't reduce trust costs. It accelerates them.

## Today's model: trust after the fact

Most insurers still:

1. Collect data
2. Store centrally
3. Verify later
4. Resolve disputes manually

Every claim becomes an investigation. Trust is applied *after* events occur.

## The shift: trust at the point of creation

SSI and KERI introduce:

- Cryptographic identities
- Signed events at creation
- Independently verifiable credentials
- Tamper-evident histories
- Machine-verifiable provenance

> Proof replaces process.

Every interaction carries cryptographic evidence of:

- Who created it
- When it happened
- What was asserted
- What was authorised

No PDFs. No screenshots. No upload portals. Just verifiable facts.

## What changes for insurance

With KERI + SSI:

- Claims become cryptographic events
- Policies become living credentials
- Risk signals arrive pre-verified
- Compliance becomes continuous
- AI agents operate safely on proofs

You stop paying repeatedly to rediscover trust. You establish it once — at the edge — and reuse it everywhere.

## Economic impact

Instead of rising trust costs:

- Manual reviews fall
- Fraud exposure drops
- Automation becomes safer
- Data pipelines become cleaner
- Compliance overhead shrinks

Trust becomes infrastructure — not labour, not paperwork, not bureaucracy.

## Bottom line

AI guarantees one thing: **the cost of establishing trust will rise dramatically** — unless insurers move from human-mediated verification to cryptographic, machine-verifiable proof.

KERI-based SSI doesn't just reduce fraud. It restructures insurance economics.

> It turns trust from an operating expense into a reusable asset.

In the age of AI, that shift may define the future of insurance.
